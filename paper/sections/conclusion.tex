\section{Conclusion}

We present a frozen evidence record for multimodal depression and Parkinson recognition under disjoint-corpus partial labels. The method preserves two independent disease logits, masks unknown labels, uses immutable TRAIN-only reliability-weighted pseudo targets only where accepted, and combines online audio/video features with availability-aware shared fusion. The full task-conditioned R4 configuration remains a secondary reference; equal-parameter shared fusion is the primary parsimonious paper candidate by a DEV-only pre-Test decision; temporal audio remains the baseline.

Five-seed DEV evidence supports a multimodal Mean advantage over audio and does not separate shared fusion from full R4 under the frozen paired interval. In the one-shot final Test report, shared fusion has the highest Mean point estimate across all three views, but all predeclared paired intervals include zero. We therefore make no Final-Test superiority claim. The ablation record supports direct pseudo-supervision, uncertainty/reliability handling, the online audio input branch, and a narrow sample-specific pseudo-alignment control; it preserves negative findings for several other mechanisms. The resulting claims concern observed-task generalization only, not clinical diagnosis, missing-label correctness, or comorbidity recovery.
